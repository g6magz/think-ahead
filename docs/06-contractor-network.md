## What the shade-system draft does say about install labor

These are planning estimates for the solar shade product, not a network agreement:

- Base installation cost: **$32,000** (planning range about $18,000–$55,000), treated as subcontractor pay in the unit-economics table.
- A suggested subcontract markup of **15–25%** (base **20%**) is already reflected in the 35% gross margin on delivered cost, not added a second time on list price.
- Colorado context used in the scale section: about **620** solar PV installers in the state (figure as cited in that draft).
- Against the placeholder volume ramp (100 systems in year 1, 1,700 in year 10):
  - Years 1–3: about **8–28 technicians**, described as small next to 620 if Think Ahead can hire or subcontract and train people on a novel system.
  - Year 10: about **136 technicians**, on the order of one-fifth of the state’s current PV-installer employment if all volume stayed in Colorado.
- The draft’s verdict: subcontracted labor can match years 1–3 in Colorado; by years 5–10, labor — not demand — is the likely constraint unless training and multi-state (Hail Alley) expansion are underway. Suggested fixes in the source include an apprenticeship / subcontractor academy, standardized site kits, and Hail Alley crew bases.

Jason’s earlier planning remark of **25% labor and 15% material** is compared in section 15 of the shade draft. That comparison is about cost share, not a contractor-network contract: the draft finds “material” as solar fabric only at 16.7%, all hardware about 36%, and all labor (factory plus field) about 22%. Planning view used there: about 36% hardware, 19% install labor, 7% factory overhead, 35% gross margin.

None of the above is a signed contractor network.
